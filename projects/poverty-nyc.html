Exploratory Analysis of Poverty in NYC

Python · scikit-learn · imbalanced-learn · Social-determinants analytics

An exploratory machine-learning study of poverty in New York City: what drives it, and which factors best predict who is at risk.

Poverty is a foundational social determinant of health, and understanding what drives it is the first step toward targeting support where it's needed. Using ~68,000 records from the NYCgov Poverty Measure (NYC Open Data), this project predicts whether an individual is in poverty and, more importantly, surfaces which factors matter most.

Approach

  • Feature selection
    Features were chosen where two independent methods — correlation and mutual information — agreed, after stripping out IDs and leakage-prone columns. This left 10 features spanning work, education, household, and language.
  • Class imbalance
    Only ~18% of the sample is in poverty. SMOTE, oversampling, and undersampling were compared, and models.
  • Model comparison
    Naive Bayes, Decision Tree, and Logistic Regression, each tested across all four resampling strategies with min-max normalization.

Results

The sharpest finding was about method, not just numbers. Left unbalanced, the best model looked 84% accurate — yet caught only about 1 in 4 people actually in poverty.

Rebalancing the data showed that recall for identifying at-risk individuals jumped from roughly 1 in 4 to 3 in 4 across every model. Logistic Regression with resampling gave the best overall balance.

ModelRecall — no resamplingRecall — with resampling
Logistic Regression0.280.76
Naive Bayes0.580.75
Decision Tree0.390.64

Factors that drive poverty: work (hours and employment) and education, while demographics like age and borough matter less.

Why it matters

In public health and policy, missing someone in need costs far more than a false alarm — so a model tuned to find vulnerable people matters more than one chasing headline accuracy. And by identifying work and education as the dominant drivers, the analysis points to where anti-poverty efforts could focus.
