Exploratory Analysis of Poverty in NYC

Python · scikit-learn · imbalanced-learn · Social-determinants analytics

A machine-learning study of which socio-economic factors most shape poverty status in New York City, using the NYCgov Poverty Measure data — with a focus on reliably identifying the people most at risk, not just maximizing overall accuracy.

Poverty is a foundational social determinant of health, and knowing what drives it is a first step toward targeting support effectively. Using ~68,000 records from NYC Open Data, this project builds classifiers to predict whether an individual is in poverty and, more importantly, surfaces which factors matter most.

Approach

  • Consensus feature selection
    Rather than hand-picking predictors, features were selected by consensus across two independent techniques — correlation and mutual information — after removing identifiers and leakage-prone columns. This yielded 10 features spanning work, education, household, and language.
  • Handling class imbalance
    Only ~18% of the sample is in poverty, so a naive model can score high accuracy while missing most at-risk people. Compared SMOTE, random oversampling, and random undersampling, and evaluated on recall and F1 — not accuracy alone.
  • Model comparison
    Naive Bayes, Decision Tree, and Logistic Regression, each across all four resampling strategies, with min-max normalization.

Key findings

Resampling was decisive. Without it, Logistic Regression reached 84% accuracy but only 0.28 recall — missing nearly three-quarters of people actually in poverty. With resampling, recall rose to ~0.76, correctly identifying most at-risk individuals. This is the core lesson: on imbalanced social data, accuracy is misleading, and optimizing to catch the vulnerable minority matters more.

Logistic Regression with resampling gave the best balance (F1 ≈ 0.52, recall ≈ 0.76), and the choice of resampling method made little difference. Work (hours and employment) and education were the strongest drivers of poverty status, while demographics like age and borough carried little signal.

Why it matters

Framed for equity: in a public-health or policy context, failing to identify someone in need (a false negative) is costlier than a false alarm — so a model tuned for recall better serves the goal of reaching vulnerable populations. The analysis points toward employment and education as leverage points for anti-poverty policy.
